# Next-level scripts: Varnik Labs, wave 2

These scripts follow what the research says is winning in October 2026 (see `reports/Viral short video trends India.md`):

- **A human in frame 1.** Open on a real-looking person or the founder, not a logo or type.
- **A specific story, not a capability list.** One business, one night, one problem.
- **Built to be sent.** Every script has a moment someone will forward to a friend who runs a shop, clinic or institute. On Instagram, sends to non-followers matter most.
- **India-native.** Hinglish where it fits, festivals, references from the street.
- **A CTA that starts a conversation:** "Comment AUDIT" (auto-DM) or click-to-WhatsApp, rather than "visit our website".
- **Realistic visuals.** Each film is built from photoreal shots (AI stills animated into 5-second clips), with our motion-graphic text layered on top. The polished type is kept, but it sits on real-looking footage.

**Legend:** `VO` = voiceover · `OST` = on-screen text · `SFX` = sound effect · `[SHOT]` = a realistic visual to generate.

## 1. "9:47 PM": cinematic micro-story (rebuild of the industry Reels)
**Format:** 9:16 · 30 s · Reels/Shorts + ads · Gia VO, cinematic score
**Why it can travel:** it reads as a tiny film. Every clinic owner has missed that call, and they'll send it to a colleague.

| Time | Visual | Audio / text |
|---|---|---|
| 0.0–2.0 | [SHOT] Extreme close-up: a phone face-down on a dark clinic counter, buzzing. It lights the desk. | SFX buzz, buzz. OST: **9:47 PM** |
| 2.0–5.0 | [SHOT] A woman in bed at night, face lit by her phone, hand on her jaw. | VO: "She called your clinic at nine forty-seven." |
| 5.0–8.0 | [SHOT] An empty reception, the landline blinking red. Slow push-in. | VO: "Nobody picked up." · SFX: the ring stops dead |
| 8.0–11.0 | [SHOT] Morning. She walks into a *different* clinic, seen over her shoulder. | VO: "By morning, she'd booked somewhere else." OST: **You never knew she called.** |
| 11.0–12.0 | Cut to black. | Silence. A single heartbeat-like hit. |
| 12.0–15.5 | [SHOT] The same dark reception. The lights snap on. | VO: "Now imagine she got a reply in ten seconds." · music drop |
| 15.5–22.0 | [SHOT] Her hand taps a phone; the booking UI animates over it. [SHOT] Morning: a man smiles at a reminder over chai. | VO: "Booked in three taps. Reminded the day before. While you slept." |
| 22.0–26.0 | [SHOT] A full, calm waiting room, receptionist smiling. | VO: "Same clinic. Fuller mornings." |
| 26.0–30.0 | [SHOT] The doctor-owner, arms crossed, looking at camera. End card. | VO: "Comment AUDIT and we'll show you how many calls you're missing." OST: **Comment AUDIT** |

**Industry swaps:** restaurant (8:30 pm Friday, empty table, a phone call nobody answered), coaching institute (fee question at 10 pm), real estate (lead goes cold over a weekend), gym (6 am class, two people). Same structure, new shots.

## 2. "I audited this restaurant in 60 seconds": founder screen-audit
**Format:** 9:16 · 45–60 s · founder voice (Sumit) over a screen recording, with face in a corner bubble
**Why it can travel:** a specific subject, a verdict and useful tips. Other owners save it and send it on.
**Ethics:** only audit businesses that have agreed, or your own clients. Never roast without permission.

| Time | Visual | Audio |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 | Face cam, then a snap zoom onto their Google listing | "This restaurant has 4.6 stars… and is losing ₹2 lakh a month. Here's why." |
| 3–15 | Screen: Google Maps listing, zoom on the opening hours (wrong) and photos (from 2019) | "Hours say closed on Sunday. They're open. Google sends people away." |
| 15–30 | Screen: Instagram grid, with red circles drawn on | "Last post: 47 days ago. Menu is a blurry photo. Nobody's ordering from that." |
| 30–45 | Screen: website loading spinner, then a stopwatch overlay at 8.2 s | "The website takes eight seconds on 4G. Half your customers left at three." |
| 45–55 | Face cam | "Three fixes, one weekend. If you want me to do this for your business, comment AUDIT." |

**Make it a series:** "Audit #01 – Clinic, Pune", "Audit #02 – Gym, Indore" and so on. A numbered series builds the habit of coming back.

## 3. "Same shop. Two versions.": before/after split
**Format:** 9:16 · 20 s · split screen · beat-synced cuts · no VO, just music and text
**Why it can travel:** the transformation pays off visually in under a second, and people watch it twice, which adds watch time.

| Time | Visual | Text |
|---|---|---|
| 0–1.5 | [SHOT] One mithai shop. The frame splits down the middle: left is dull and empty, right is the same shop, glowing and crowded | **Same shop.** |
| 1.5–3 | Left: a faded hand-painted board. Right: a clean new logo sign | **Same sweets.** |
| 3–8 | Beat-synced pairs: old Instagram vs new grid · WhatsApp "hello??" left on read vs instant catalogue reply · Google 3.1★ vs 4.7★ | **Different brand.** |
| 8–14 | Right side takes over the full frame: a Diwali rush, boxes flying off the counter | **Different Diwali.** |
| 14–20 | End card | **Which side is your shop on?** · Comment AUDIT |

**Variants:** a salon, an optician, a coaching centre, a cloud kitchen. Use real client before/afters as soon as you have permission.

## 4. "Dukaan Digital Yojna 2026": Diwali satire (Hinglish)
**Format:** 9:16 · 35 s · parody of a government scheme ad · posting deadline **before 1 Nov** (Diwali is 8 Nov)
**Why it can travel:** "Yojna" satire is a live meme format in India. It's funny, seasonal and very sendable.

| Time | Visual | Audio |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 | Mock government-ad look: tricolour-style wipe, a stiff announcer frame, a fake "Doordarshan" style lower third. **Keep it clearly parody: no real emblems or ministry names.** | Announcer VO (formal Hindi): "Pradhan Dukaan Digital Yojna… 2026!" |
| 3–10 | [SHOT] A shopkeeper (uncle) yawning behind the counter, phone buzzing unanswered | "Is Diwali, har dukaan ko milega… ek Google profile jo sach mein khulta hai." |
| 10–18 | Comic checklist pops up: ✔ WhatsApp reply in 10 seconds ✔ Photos from *this* decade ✔ A website that opens before the chai gets cold | VO reads them deadpan · SFX government-stamp "THUNK" on each |
| 18–26 | [SHOT] The same uncle, now beaming, phone full of orders, family packing boxes | "Laabh: zyada customer. Nuksaan: kuch nahi. Bas thoda sa sharam ki aapka competitor pehle kar gaya." |
| 26–35 | End card in a stamped "official form" style | "Aavedan karne ke liye: comment karein **DIWALI**." · fine print: *Yeh ek sarkari yojna nahi hai. Varnik Labs hai.* |

## 5. "Do dukaanein, ek gali" (Two shops, one street): micro-drama series
**Format:** 9:16 · 3 episodes × 45 s · recurring characters · cinematic photoreal
**Why it can travel:** micro-dramas are the fastest-growing format in India (FICCI-EY projects ₹2,300 crore by 2028). A cliffhanger brings people back, and recurring characters build followers.

**Characters:** **Sharma ji** (old-school sweet shop, stubborn, lovable) and **Neha** (the new café across the street). Sharma ji's son **Rohan** wants to modernise.

- **Ep 1, "Khaali counter":** Diwali week. Neha's café has a queue; Sharma ji's counter is empty. Rohan shows him Neha's Instagram: 10,000 followers. Sharma ji: *"Followers se mithai nahi bikti."* Cliffhanger: a customer walks in and asks Sharma ji, *"Aapka WhatsApp number kya hai? Order karna tha."* He doesn't have one.
- **Ep 2, "Ek din ka challenge":** Rohan secretly calls Varnik Labs. Montage: product shoot of the kaju katli, a Google profile, a WhatsApp catalogue. Sharma ji catches him. Cliffhanger: *"Ek din. Agar ek bhi order nahi aaya, sab band."*
- **Ep 3, "Pehla order":** Morning. Silence. Then: ping. Ping. Ping-ping-ping. Sharma ji pretends not to smile. Final shot: he and Neha nod at each other across the street. End card: **Har dukaan ki kahani badal sakti hai.** · Comment AUDIT

## 6. "Your next customer is in this video": personalised prospect video
**Format:** 9:16 or 1:1 · 25 s · rendered one per prospect by the pipeline · sent on WhatsApp/DM
**Why it works:** vendors report 2–3x the replies of plain text (vendor data, not independent).

| Time | Visual | Audio |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 | Founder face cam | "Hi {Owner name}, this is Sumit from Varnik Labs. I made this just for {Business name}." |
| 3–12 | Their actual Google listing and Instagram screenshots, with two red circles and one green tick | Gia VO or founder: "Your reviews are great. But {specific issue 1} and {specific issue 2}." |
| 12–20 | A mock-up of *their* name on a new homepage or Instagram grid (motion graphic) | "Here's what {Business name} could look like in two weeks." |
| 20–25 | Founder face | "Reply YES and I'll send the full audit, free." |

## 7. "Website isn't the problem": founder myth-buster for LinkedIn
**Format:** 4:5 or 9:16 · 40 s · Sumit talking to camera in one take, with burned-in captions · personal profile, not the company page (personal profiles get about 1.6x the engagement)

> "Most business owners think they need a new website. (beat) They don't.
> I've audited 40 businesses this year. 31 of them had a fine website.
> What they didn't have: a reply in under five minutes.
> A customer who messages you at 9 PM and hears back at 11 AM has already paid someone else.
> Fix the reply before you fix the design. If you want to know your reply time, comment REPLY and I'll test your business tonight."

*(Use real numbers from your own audits; the 40/31 above are placeholders.)*
